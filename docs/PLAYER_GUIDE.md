## Before you begin

Follow the [README installation steps](../MOD_README.md) and back up any save you
care about before starting or updating the mod. Generic Mod Config Menu is
optional, but it is the easiest way to change controls, encounter pacing,
capture assistance, combat, stamina, chores, fainting, accessibility options,
and villager partners.

Pelipper Town follows Stardew Valley's selected language. French and Vietnamese
include the complete mod interface plus localized species, move, ability, type,
and category names. Additional translations can provide the same coverage;
change Stardew's language and restart the game to switch.

The base mod includes 541 Pokémon definitions. Content packs can add more, so
the number in your save may be higher. Use **Partner Journal → Wild Pokémon**
when looking for a species: that page reads the current year, season, weather,
time, farm type, fossil research, settings, and installed content packs from
your game.

## How Pelipper Town fits together

The mod is built around one connected loop:

1. **Explore** a habitat and discover its current wild population.
2. **Battle or catch** to grow the farmer's skills and the Pokémon's level.
3. **Care for and configure** a six-member party in the Partner Journal.
4. **Work and travel** with a deployed partner whose species, level, command,
   and chore policies decide what it can safely do.
5. **Research, breed, and complete stories** to broaden the roster, unlock
   recipes, and open special encounters.
6. **Build specialized teams** whose moves, passives, Type Affinities, field
   skills, and personalities support different parts of Stardew Valley.

Several progress tracks advance at the same time, but they are not
interchangeable:

| Progress | Belongs to | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pokémon level, moves, bond, health, energy, and work history | One Pokémon | Combat strength, field tier, evolution, reliability, and daily condition |
| Training, Battling, and Breeding | One farmer | Recipes, bonuses, professions, and skill perks |
| Pokédex catches, milestones, party, and personal storage | One farmer in the save | Collection progress and permanent personal rewards |
| Trainer ladders, museum research, outbreaks, and most active encounters | The farm world | Shared opportunities and world progression |
| Farm Nuzlocke choice and Memorial | The farm | Shared challenge rules and retired Pokémon history |
| Nuzlocke daily catch allowance | One farmer | One successful ordinary wild catch per farmer and in-game day |
| Team Box | The farm | Storage shared by everyone on that farm |
| Global Box | The local player profile | Storage that follows that player between different saves and farms |

This distinction matters most in multiplayer. A farmhand can build a different
party and skill path from the host, while both players still see the same
host-simulated wild population and help with the same visiting-trainer fight.

If the guide and your save disagree, use the Partner Journal. Its Wild Pokémon,
Skills, Milestones, Summary, Chores, and Evolution pages are calculated from
the data and settings loaded in that game.

## Default controls

All mod controls can be changed through Generic Mod Config Menu. Controller
labels below use the Xbox layout. On other controllers, use the button in the
same physical position.

| Action | Keyboard | Controller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open Partner Journal | `P` | `L3` |
| Recall or redeploy active Pokémon | `R` | Tap `R3` |
| Select next party Pokémon | `]` | No default binding |
| Select previous party Pokémon | `[` | No default binding |
| Send or swap to queued Pokémon | `O` | No default binding |
| Aim and throw a Poké Ball | Hold and release `G` | Select a Ball and hold/release Action/Check |
| Open the breeding menu while inside a Barn | `B` | `RB` |

L3 opens the Partner Journal immediately; holding R3 is also available. Journal
and breeding menus support the stick or D-pad for movement, the normal Confirm
button to choose, and the normal Cancel button to go back or close.
The breeding shortcut only takes over `RB` while the player is inside a Barn,
so it does not interfere with ordinary shoulder-button use elsewhere.

Recall and switching are separate actions. `R` only puts the active Pokémon in
its Ball or sends that same Pokémon back out. The bracket keys only move the
gray queued portrait; tap `O` afterward to recall the current partner and
deploy the queued one. Hold `O` to open the circular party wheel, aim with the
mouse or movement controls, and release to send the highlighted Pokémon. A
bound controller send/swap button uses the same tap-or-hold behavior with the
D-pad or either stick. Open **Mod Options → Pelipper Town → Companions** to
rebind any keyboard or controller control.

## Starting your journey

Each farmer chooses a journey independently. On a not-yet-onboarded save, the
journey setup menu opens shortly after the world loads, once the player can
move and no event or dialogue is active. After choosing, leave the farmhouse
to meet Marnie and Miltank and complete the selected opening. Your options are:

| Journey | What you receive |
| --- | --- |
| Starters | Choose three regions, then choose one Grass, one Fire, and one Water starter from those regions |
| Pikachu or Eevee | Choose one of the two as your first partner |
| Bootstraps | Start without a Pokémon, but receive ten Poké Balls and Marnie's Ranch Guide |
| Hardcore | Start without a Pokémon or free Poké Balls, but receive the Ranch Guide |
| Custom | Choose one to three Pokémon from the currently installed roster |

All modes except Hardcore receive the normal starter Poké Ball grant. Potion
and Poké Ball recipes are available from the beginning. If your inventory is
full, pending supplies wait safely until there is room.

Each Pokémon Marnie gives you has an independent 50/50 chance of being male or
female, including Pikachu, Eevee, and Custom partners. Genderless and single-sex
species keep their normal gender. This applies to new starting partners;
existing Pokémon keep their saved gender, and catches and eggs use species odds.

Your choice affects only the opening. It does not close off wild species,
quests, skills, bosses, or later progression.

### Optional Farm Nuzlocke

During the host's first journey setup, a dedicated screen asks about **Farm
Nuzlocke** after the host chooses their start. This is one decision for the
save, not a separate farmer setting: all current and future farmhands inherit
it while the challenge is active. Choosing Back returns to the previous starter
screen without committing either choice.

- Each farmer gets one successful ordinary wild-Pokémon catch per in-game day.
  One farmer's catch does not spend another's. Failed catches do not spend it.
- Boss encounters and legendary Pokémon are exempt and do not spend the daily
  ordinary catch.
- A partner that reaches zero HP in wild, monster, story, or trainer combat is
  retired after any available faint-prevention ability resolves.
  Consensual player-versus-player battles do not retire Pokémon.
- Retired Pokémon enter the farm's shared, read-only Pokémon Memorial and keep
  their original trainer attribution. They cannot be withdrawn while the
  challenge remains active.
- Every farmer receives a placeable red-and-white Memorial gravestone after
  onboarding. If a backpack is full, delivery retries when space is available.
- If the host's health reaches zero and they wake at Harvey's clinic, the
  farm-wide run ends automatically. Every memorialized Pokémon is fully revived
  and returned to its original Trainer's Pokémon Chest, and the result is posted
  to each online farmer's chat history. A farmhand knockout leaves the challenge
  active by default, even if that farmer has no usable Pokémon left.
- The host can enable **Any farmer ends Nuzlocke** on the Companions page of Mod
  Options to make the same failure and restoration apply when any farmhand is
  knocked out.
- Each farmer's Pokédex and Trainer Milestones remain personal. Shared research
  and story gates still use the farm's collective discovery state.
- The host can use **End Farm Nuzlocke** at any placed Memorial. Confirming a
  second time disables the challenge, fully revives every memorialized Pokémon,
  and returns each one to its original Trainer's personal Pokémon Chest. The
  SMAPI console fallback is `pokemon_nuzlocke off`.

If Pelipper Town's custom menu text is too small, open **Mod Options →
Appearance** and increase **Pokémon text size** from its 100% default. The
setting updates Journey Setup, the Partner Journal, and custom hover details.

## Your first catches

Wild Pokémon begin gently during Spring of Year 1 when first-year scaling is
enabled. More of each habitat opens as the year and save progress advance.

1. Walk through an outdoor area until a wild Pokémon appears.
2. Send out a partner with `O` if you want help weakening it.
3. Hold `G` to aim a Poké Ball, then release to throw.
4. Lower the target's health to improve the catch chance.
5. Use direct hits, back hits, sleep, stronger Balls, and Training bonuses when
   a species is difficult to catch.

Poké Balls, Great Balls, and Ultra Balls use 1x, 1.5x, and 2x capture strength.
Your companion will not reduce a shiny wild Pokémon below 10 percent HP. The
**Catching** combat stance applies the same safety floor to every wild target;
at that floor, companions keep using moves without dealing more damage. Sleep,
Paralysis, Freeze, and other non-damaging effects remain available, while Poison
and Burn are suppressed. **Peaceful** ignores ordinary combat entirely.

A Shiny announces itself with a distinct positional chime and keeps an
exclamation marker above its head for as long as it remains wild and visible.

Ordinary wild residents remain on their map until caught, defeated, or the day
ends. Dungeon residents reset when you leave their floor.

After the Sewer becomes accessible, it has its own level 24–41 Poison/Ghost
table rather than borrowing outdoor encounters. Its Mutant Bug Lair entrance
leads to a level 25–44 insect-focused table, including rare Scyther and Pinsir.
Check **Partner Journal → Wild Pokémon** for the live, complete list and any
active progression filters.

Metapod and Kakuna now have low-weight daytime Forest encounters, with rare
Butterfree and Beedrill there as well as on Ginger Island. Every Eeveelution
also has a rare thematic wild route: Vaporeon at the Beach, Jolteon at the
Railroad, Flareon in the Desert, Espeon in the Mountains, Umbreon and Leafeon
in the Secret Woods, Glaceon in winter Mountain/Railroad pools, and Sylveon in
the Forest. Their seasons and day/night windows still apply.

The Quarry Mine adds a level 32–51 Rock, Ground, and Steel route, including
Gible; Witch's
Swamp carries a compact level 34–50 Dark, Poison, and Ghost pool after its
story access unlocks; and Ginger Island North Cave has a small 31–48 fossil
table. These are explicit maps only—homes, shops, shrines, and other utility
interiors do not receive ambient Pokémon.

### How wild ecology chooses an encounter

Finding a Pokémon is a sequence of checks rather than one global random table:

1. The current map is matched to an authored habitat zone or dungeon band.
2. Year, season, time, weather, terrain, farm type, fossil research, installed
   integrations, and settings remove ineligible entries.
3. Lures, Slates, rarity, and chain effects adjust the remaining
   choices without making an otherwise ineligible species valid.
4. Evolution stages are grouped by family and no family can naturally own more
   than 35 percent of an ambient roll; excess chance flows to other eligible
   families. A chosen DexNav hunt or the announced outbreak preference applies
   afterward and may deliberately exceed that ceiling.
5. The map must have capacity and a safe, reachable placement tile with the
   required terrain.
6. The selected species receives its authored temperament and moves for its
   level, then remains in the location until resolved or cleared by the area's
   normal lifecycle.

This is why waiting longer is not always the answer. If the Journal lists a
species but none appear, check its season, time, weather, terrain, and research
conditions before increasing encounter frequency. Incense accelerates valid
spawn opportunities; a Slate favors a valid type; neither overrides the
eligibility rules.

### Regional forms

When an eligible Rattata, Raticate, Raichu, Sandshrew, Sandslash, Vulpix,
Ninetales, Diglett, Dugtrio, Meowth, Persian, Geodude, Graveler, Golem, Grimer,
Muk, Exeggutor, or Marowak is selected, it has a one-in-three chance to be its
Alolan form. The same rule produces Galarian Ponyta, Rapidash, Slowpoke,
Slowbro, Slowking, Weezing, Articuno, Zapdos, or Moltres
from the matching base-species encounter. This preserves the base species'
habitat, season, time, level, rarity, and story conditions instead of creating
a second ecology entry.

Alolan Incense and Galarian Incense raise the matching chance to two in three
for the current outdoor area through the end of the day. They do not increase
spawn frequency, make an ineligible species valid, or affect the other region.
The first Alolan Pokémon a player catches teaches that player the Alolan
Incense recipe; their first Galarian catch independently teaches Galarian
Incense. Alolan Incense costs 20 Sap, 10 Fiber, and 1 Coconut; Galarian Incense
costs 20 Sap, 10 Fiber, and 1 Wool. Alolan Incense uses a tropical
turquoise-and-coral icon; Galarian Incense uses royal blue and magenta. Both
finished incenses are also sold by Pierre for 1,500g.

Regional evolution routes are:

- Alolan Rattata evolves at level 20 at night; Alolan Sandshrew and Vulpix can
  use an Ice Stone; Alolan Diglett evolves at 26, Alolan Geodude at 25,
  Graveler at 32, Grimer at 38, and Meowth at 80 bond.
- Galarian Ponyta evolves at level 40. Galarian Slowpoke uses the mod's compact
  replacement for its unavailable Galarica items: level 20 during the day
  gives Slowbro and level 20 at night gives Slowking.
- Hisuian Sneasel appears during the day on the Mountain, Railroad, and Island
  North in fall and winter. It evolves into Sneasler at level 30 during the
  day, and rare hostile Sneasler can appear independently in the same areas.
- Cubone evolves into Alolan Marowak at level 28 at night on the expanded Beach
  or Ginger Island. Away from those locations, level 28 still gives regular
  Marowak.
- Galarian Farfetch'd and Mr. Mime are excluded for now because Sirfetch'd's
  required attack animation and Mr. Rime's overworld sheet are not complete in
  the pinned SpriteCollab revision.

The older regional families keep their existing bespoke rules. Pikachu and
Exeggcute evolve into their Alolan results on the expanded Beach or Ginger
Island, by level or their usual Stone; outside those locations they evolve
normally. Paldean Wooper is an independent Forest riverlands night encounter
in spring through fall and evolves into Clodsire at level 20; Clodsire can also
appear there at higher levels. Hisuian Zorua appears at night in the Backwoods,
Woods, and Railroad in fall and winter, evolves at level 30, and Hisuian
Zoroark can also appear independently in the same areas.

### Yamask and Cofagrigus

Yamask appears in the Calico Desert at night and on Skull Cavern floors 1–40.
It evolves into Cofagrigus at level 34. Cofagrigus can also appear directly in
the Desert at night and on Skull Cavern floors 21–80, becoming more common in
the middle depth bands before growing rarer deeper down.

### Rookidee and Buizel families

Rookidee appears on all farm layouts, at the Bus Stop, and on the Mountain
by day in every season, at levels 3–8. Corvisquire is rarer on the Mountain
and Railroad at levels 18–26; Corviknight is a rare Mountain
and Railroad encounter at levels 38–46. Both evolutions retain the daytime,
year-round schedule. Rookidee evolves at level 18, then Corvisquire at level 38.
Rookidee and Corvisquire's **Big Pecks** protects crops from overnight crows
while either is healthy in the host farmer's traveling party, even inside its
Ball. Fainted or stored Pokémon do not provide protection.
Corviknight's **Pressure** makes enemy Pokémon
take 50% longer between attacks.

Buizel appears along freshwater shores in Cindersap Forest and the Mountain
in spring, summer, and fall, at levels 5–10 and any time of day. Floatzel is a
rarer shore encounter in those areas and on Island West at levels 26–34.
Buizel evolves at level 26. Their **Swift Swim** works during rain: Buizel
shortens fish bite times by 15% and adds 0.25 outdoor movement speed; Floatzel
shortens bite times by 25% and adds 0.5 outdoor movement speed. Both can water,
forage, fish, and collect crab pots; Floatzel can also transport machine output.

### Habitat chains, outbreaks, and the DexNav

Every farmer maintains one active shiny chain for an authored habitat zone.
Catching or defeating a wild Pokémon advances that chain. Resolving a wild
encounter in another zone starts a new chain there.

- Base shiny odds are 1 in 2,048.
- Every ten chain results adds another independent shiny roll.
- A shiny appearance spends one completed ten-result rung instead of erasing
  the whole chain.
- Defeats after the first ten also increase local refill speed, up to 4x after
  70 defeats. The defeat total keeps growing after that; every ten defeats
  beyond 20 pulls the local refill ring one tile closer, down to 3–5 tiles.
- Larger chains can produce small groups while still respecting map capacity.

Authored wild bosses use the same roll when they are not explicitly forced
shiny or non-shiny. The chain for the boss's exact habitat zone therefore
applies to the Four Signs, solstice visitors such as Solgaleo, the seasonal
legendary birds, and ordinary relic summons. Trainer-owned Pokémon are not wild
encounters and do not use shiny chains.

Each morning has a 24 percent chance to start an outbreak. An outbreak selects
one currently eligible outdoor species and location, records the announcement
in the in-game text history so its location can be checked again, and creates 8
to 12 same-level members during that day. The announced group is intentional,
and the outbreak's 8x preference applies after normal ecology balancing, so its
family can exceed the ordinary 35-percent ceiling for that day.

### Paradox and Ultra Space days

At six hearts, Maru mails one **Paradox Emitter** and teaches its recipe: 1
Electirizer, 1 Battery Pack, and 5 Refined Quartz. Using it chooses an ancient
Scarlet or future Violet signal for tomorrow. The next day is tinted to match,
and each ordinary ambient wild spawn has a 30 percent chance to be replaced by
a level 35-50 Paradox Pokémon. This applies anywhere ordinary wild Pokémon can
spawn. Species missing from that farmer's Pokédex have four times the selection
weight. Scarlet days also place a passive level-60 Walking Wake in Cindersap
Forest; Violet days place a passive level-60 Iron Leaves in the Secret Woods.
Either special encounter fights only after being provoked.

At two hearts, Leo offers a recipe he found buried in the sand in exchange for
three bird Pokémon to join his parrot family. Articuno, Zapdos, and Moltres,
including their Galarian forms, count toward this request. The three birds can
be the same species. After the third separate donation, he gives you the
**Ultra Space Beacon** recipe: 1 Electirizer, 1 Battery
Pack, 5 Void Essence, and 10 Cinder Shards. Its next-day purple anomaly gives
each ordinary ambient wild spawn a 30 percent chance to be replaced by a level
45-55 Ultra Beast, with the same uncaught-species bias.

Only one farm-wide anomaly can be scheduled at a time. Both devices are
consumed on successful use. The full encounter pools and art audit are recorded
in `ANOMALY_EVENTS.md`.

The DexNav is unlocked at Training 4 or awarded by Maru's Porygon favor. Keep
it anywhere in your backpack to see screen-edge arrows toward every off-screen
wild Pokémon. Use it from the toolbar to scan the current habitat zone's
seasonal species and estimated spawn chances. On multi-zone maps such as Town,
the header names the zone under the player and the list excludes species from
other parts of the map. Caught species appear normally; uncaught species are
gray. Species without a valid placement tile on the current map or dungeon
floor are removed before the displayed odds are calculated. One species can be
hunted at a time, beginning at four times its encounter weight. Each successful
non-target ambient spawn raises that preference; after 20 such misses, the next
feasible ambient spawn is guaranteed to be the hunted species. Changing or
clearing the target resets the streak. Any number of species can be protected
from companion damage below 10 percent HP. Spawned hunted species use
double-size red arrows, or double-size purple arrows when shiny; other shiny
targets retain blue arrows.

## The Partner Journal

Press `P` to open the Journal. This is where you manage most of the mod:

- review and reorder the six-member traveling party;
- send out, recall, nickname, evolve, summarize, or sell a Pokémon;
- choose commands, combat stance, moves, and chore policies;
- inspect HP, energy, bond, stats, abilities, types, and work history;
- track Training, Battling, Breeding, and milestones;
- browse the loaded Pokédex and habitat-aware Wild Pokémon pages; and
- review evolution routes and their exact requirements.

Extra catches go to your personal Pokémon Chest storage. A placed Pokémon Chest
opens party and storage management. The Journal shows the traveling party;
open a Chest to see stored Pokémon. The Chest, Team Box, and Global Box each
have a search field with a **Name / Passive** toggle. Name search matches both
nicknames and species names; Passive search matches passive ability names.
The Team and Global Box also have persistent **Deposit**, **Withdraw**, **Sell**,
and **Favorite** modes: choose the action first, then click Pokémon until that
mode is cancelled or changed. Favorite uses the existing star and protects the
Pokémon from sales; selecting Favorite again toggles it off.

The **Skills** page lists all ten Training, Battling, and Breeding level perks
plus every level-5 and level-10 profession branch. Selecting a level-5-or-higher
skill can retrain only that skill's professions for 10,000g; its level and XP
are preserved.

The morning after you successfully catch your seventh Pokémon, Mom mails a
separate reusable red **Pokémon Journal**. Older saves recover credit from their
recorded wild catches; qualifying players receive the letter the morning after
loading the update. Hold it and
press the action button to browse the complete loaded collection. Its sort
control cycles through National Pokédex, regional, rideable-first, and type
order. Its filter cycles between all, caught, uncaught, and Shiny species.
Hover over a Pokémon to see its passive, where it can be found, whether it can
be ridden, and whether that ride can fly or swim. A normal entry has a dark
outline. Once you catch a Shiny species, its shiny party picture and orange
outline permanently replace the regular picture in every view. Click the
region control repeatedly to choose all regions or Kanto through Paldea,
including Hisui as its own collection.

The Chest's **Saved Teams** menu has six numbered tabs. Each tab can have a
custom name and stores up to six party members plus the active-partner choice.
The menu places the current party beside the selected saved team, so you can
review the difference before choosing **Save Current Party** or **Use Team**.
Saving over a tab keeps its custom name. Pokémon currently locked into Barn
breeding cannot be recalled from storage as part of a team.

## Building and caring for a party

Your party holds six Pokémon and one may be deployed at a time. Use `R` to
recall or redeploy the active partner without changing it. Use `[` and `]` to
preview party members in the gray HUD portrait, then `O` to send or swap to the
queued partner. You can instead hold `O`, point at any healthy party member in
the circular wheel, and release to send that Pokémon directly.

The four movement commands serve different purposes:

| Command | Best use |
| --- | --- |
| Follow | Close formation and travel |
| Assist | Stay near you while helping with local work |
| Explore and assist | Search the broadest area for allowed work |
| Wait | Remain in place until called |

Combat stances control how readily the deployed partner engages. Defensive is
the safer everyday option. Aggressive seeks fights. Catching avoids finishing
wild targets, and Peaceful ignores ordinary combat. Optional keyboard and
controller bindings in Mod Options cycle these stances without opening the
Partner Journal.

### Health, energy, food, and bond

- HP is lost in combat. A fainted partner returns to its ball.
- Energy is spent on field work. Below 5 percent energy, movement slows and
  move cooldowns double.
- Every Pokémon wakes with full HP and energy.
- Potion, Super Potion, Revive, Antidote, Full Heal, Ether, and Max Ether cover
  the main recovery needs.
- Harvey can heal the whole party. The first successful treatment is free and
  later treatments cost 250g until his clinic favor makes care free.
- A Pokémon Healing Station restores the party up to three times per day.
- Offering ordinary Stardew food restores the same positive health and energy
  values it would give the farmer and passes along useful food buffs.
- The first direct interaction with a deployed partner each day gives bond.

Each deployed Pokémon contributes its own passive and one Type Affinity. Some
party abilities also help while their owner is not the active field partner.
The Summary and Type Affinity pages explain the exact effect and scope.

### PokéLink Station and Poké Flute allies

The **PokéLink Station** recipe is available from the beginning and costs 100
Wood, 100 Stone, and 5 Copper Bars. It can only be placed and used on the farm.
Interact with it to open its Pokémon Box-style menu and choose one eligible
Pokémon from your personal storage. Each farmer can establish up to three
successful connections per day, with only one PokéLink support Pokémon deployed
at a time. Opening the menu shows the number of connections remaining.

PokéLink support follows and assists alongside the normal active party Pokémon.
Its HP and energy remain attached to the boxed Pokémon, but it does not add
permanent HP or energy bars to the HUD. Its allied health bar appears during
combat. Its passive abilities and Type Affinity do not apply while linked.
Between fights, the support Pokémon communicates the lower of its
remaining HP and energy through the Psychic Mind Reader reaction set:

| Remaining condition | Reaction |
| ---: | --- |
| 80% | Heart |
| 60% | Happy |
| 40% | Ellipsis |
| 20% | Unhappy |
| 0% | Hated/angry, then return to the Pokémon Box |

The zero-condition reaction remains visible briefly before the Pokémon
disappears. Feeding can raise its condition again; crossing a lower
milestone afterward produces that reaction again.

Hold ordinary restorative food and use Action/Check while facing the PokéLink
support Pokémon, or right-click it, to restore the same positive HP and energy
the food provides and apply eligible food buffs. If the linked species is
normally rideable, face it and use Action/Check or right-click it while
not holding food to mount it. Linked Pokémon still use their moves, stats, and
normal ride eligibility; only their passive and Type Affinity are suppressed.

The **Poké Flute** is currently retired from normal player progression and has
no player-facing recipe or gift source. It remains available through developer
item commands for testing. Use the flute to fire a musical note. Hitting an
ordinary wild Pokémon recruits it until the day ends: it follows, assists, can
be fed to restore HP and energy, can be ridden, and remains catchable. Bosses
and legendary Pokémon ignore the melody. Only one flute ally can be active at
a time; recruiting another ends the old alliance and makes the previous
Pokémon hostile again.
Rideable flute allies can be mounted with the same facing Action/Check or
right-click interaction as a party or PokéLink mount. Feed flute allies with
the same held-food interaction used for party and PokéLink Pokémon. They use
the same HP/energy milestone reactions shown above, including the final angry
emote before fainting or exhaustion ends the alliance.

## Passives and Type Affinities

Party building has three effect layers, so a Pokémon can be useful even when it
is not the strongest battler or worker.

| Effect layer | When it works | Typical role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field passive | That Pokémon is deployed and conscious | Active exploration, combat, gathering, or utility |
| Team passive | A conscious Pokémon with the passive is anywhere in the six-member party | Background farming, fishing, crafting, ranching, friendship, or economy support |
| Type Affinity | The deployed conscious Pokémon's primary type; Normal/Flying uses Flying | A small universal bonus such as light, magnetism, food efficiency, crop help, or mine utility |

Mew's Protean grants every Type Affinity while Mew is deployed. Arceus receives
the same affinity coverage while deployed in addition to its displayed
Creator's Bounty passive. Fainted and stored Pokémon grant no passive effects.
Several copies of the same named passive contribute only the strongest tier,
while differently named effects can work together. In multiplayer, every
farmer's party effects apply only to that farmer.

### The 18 Type Affinities

The deployed Pokémon normally grants the affinity of its first listed type.
Normal/Flying Pokémon use Flying instead. These are separate from the named
passive shown on the Pokémon's Summary page.

| Type | Affinity | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Good Appetite | Food restores 10% more health and energy to the farmer, except items that already refill them completely. |
| Fire | Lantern Body | The deployed partner or mount casts a larger Glow Ring-like light at night and in mines, dungeons, and caves. |

## Farm work and travel

Partners can water, till, harvest, forage, fish, mine, clear brush, chop wood,
tend animals, gather orchard fruit, collect crab pots, and move machine output.
The exact list depends on species, level, field tier, job policy, and settings.

### Choosing who does what

Open a Pokémon's Chores page in the Partner Journal. It shows every job that
species can perform, its current field tier, and the maximum tier authored for
that capability. Each available job has one of three policies:

| Policy | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Off | Never take this kind of job |
| Assist | Take it while you are actively working nearby or have directed the partner to help |
| Auto | May also find safe independent jobs when the current movement command permits autonomous work |

Movement commands decide how far the selected policy may operate. `Follow`
stays close and performs only very local safe favors. `Assist` works in the
nearby help radius. `Explore and assist` searches the widest configured work
radius. `Wait` performs no chores. Destructive or player-led work still needs
the matching player signal even when its policy is `Auto`.

The practical starting setup is `Assist` for work you want to supervise,
`Auto` for harmless routines such as watering, harvesting, animal care, and
orchard collection, and `Off` for anything you do not want that Pokémon to
touch.

### What starts each chore

| Chore | How it starts and what the partner does |
| --- | --- |
| Harvest | Finds ready crops, checks that delivery will be safe, harvests them, then carries the produce back to you. Crop harvesting disables this chore globally; Protect giant crops excludes only giant-capable crop types |
| Water | Waters dry tilled soil. Higher tiers use wider watering-can footprints without watering outside valid soil |
| Forage | Collects forage, shakes ripe seasonal berry bushes, and picks up loose ground items, then carries them to you |
| Clear Brush | After you use a scythe, clears weeds inside the recent directional work area. It can also clear grass when Protect grazing grass is disabled |
| Chop Wood | After an axe signal, exact trees, stumps, and large logs must be individually targeted. Chopping one small stick also authorizes nearby sticks in that work area |
| Mine | After you use a pickaxe, breaks nearby signaled stones and boulders through Stardew's normal mining transaction, preserving normal drops |
| Tilling | After you mark a valid row or rectangle with the hoe, fills the approved plan and never spills beyond it |
| Fishing | While your cast is waiting, makes cooldown-based helper attempts and carries successful catches back |
| Animal Care | Tends eligible animals and collects available animal products |
| Orchard Care | Collects ripe fruit from fruit trees |
| Crab Pots | Collects ready pots. Higher tiers can also use bait from your inventory to reset them |
| Transport | Collects completed machine output and carries it to a matching chest filter |

Harvest, forage, fishing, and transport cargo is inventory-safe. A host-owned
partner waits with the item if your backpack or destination chest is full. A
farmhand's partner drops player-bound cargo at that farmhand's feet so the host
never mutates a remote backpack. The Journal's **Collect carried items** command
forces the same safe handoff; if a local backpack is full, it drops the cargo
instead. Partners can continue cargo-neutral chores while waiting.

### Tilling plans

Tilling uses a simple plan so a partner cannot redesign the farm by guessing.
Create one of these patterns with your hoe:

1. Till two isolated endpoints no more than eight tiles apart to request a
   straight row. Diagonal rows add a one-tile overlap so the soil remains
   connected.
2. Till four corners with exactly two X coordinates and two Y coordinates to
   request a rectangle up to eight tiles per side.

The whole proposal is rejected if it crosses water, flooring, an object, a
crop, a building, a special tile, or terrain that cannot be dug. Higher tiers
may use charged hoe footprints, but every affected tile is clipped to the
accepted row or rectangle.

### Field tiers, energy, and bond

| Pokémon level | Field tier | Example watering footprint |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 to 14 | Basic | 1 tile |
| 15 to 24 | Copper | 3-tile line |
| 25 to 39 | Steel | 5-tile line |
| 40 to 59 | Gold | 3 by 3 |
| 60+ | Iridium | 3 by 6 |

A species may have a lower authored ceiling for an individual capability. The
30-family Everstone milestone removes authored field ceilings, while the
all-species chore and all-species Iridium settings provide broader overrides.

Every finished job costs energy. With the default settings, most jobs cost 2,
breaking a stick or ordinary stone costs 3, and trees, large logs, stumps, and
boulders cost 4. A partner below the minimum energy will not start another job.
Below 5 percent energy, it moves more slowly and its action cooldowns are
longer. All Pokémon wake with full energy the next day.

Completed chores award experience and bond to the Pokémon. Every successfully
completed chore also awards the farmer's matching Stardew skill: Farming,
Fishing, Foraging, or Mining. Native rewards are preserved in full; chores
which normally give no skill experience award one point instead, capped at 20
routine points per matching Stardew skill each day. Naturally XP-bearing jobs
still preserve their normal reward rather than consuming that cap. Assisted
fish catches and monster defeats count for quests and tracked accomplishments.
For each successful helper roll, the partner samples up to five eligible
catches and prefers the first species you have caught before. If none of those
samples are familiar, it brings back the first eligible catch, so new species
remain possible without dominating automated fishing. Fishing cooldowns belong
to the farmer, so changing partners or commands cannot reset them. A long cast
can allow another attempt after the cooldown. The Pokémon's internal fishing
tool gains a tier every 20 levels, and each tier shortens that recovery by half
a second. Each job adds one bond, up to 12 bond from chores per day. At
Training level 1, the Soothe Bell recipe provides an early faster path: keep
the reusable bell in your backpack to gain two bond per chore with no daily
chore cap, and twice the normal bond from daily affection.
Better bond allows longer work bursts and shorter breaks, so a familiar partner
becomes steadier without bypassing its permissions, energy, safety checks, or
field tier.

### Crab pots and machine transport

At Copper tier, a Crab Pot helper can draw bait from your inventory to reset an
empty pot. At Steel tier or above, it can collect a catch and rebait the pot in
the same visit.

For machine transport, place a normal or Big Chest directly beside a machine
and leave one example of each desired output inside. Existing stacks are exact
filters for item, quality, and preserve type. To allow longer routes, open the
chest and enable the Pokémon Transport checkbox. A partner locks onto one
output and destination, gathers matching reachable stacks up to 999 items, and
makes one deposit. A locked or full chest waits safely. If the destination is
removed, the partner returns the cargo to you instead.

### Chore safety settings

Turn off Crop harvesting if you want ripe crops left for manual harvesting;
watering and every other chore remain available. Leave Protect giant crops on
to protect only crop types that can form giant crops, including types added by
other mods; ordinary ripe crops remain eligible. Keep destructive chores
disabled while learning the system if you do not want a partner cutting trees
or changing placed plans. Harmless jobs can remain on. Separate settings can
protect ordinary trees, fruit trees, tapped trees, grazing grass, and blocked
indoor locations. Grazing grass protection is on by default and excludes grass
from both direct targets and a helper's scythe arc; weeds remain eligible.
Targets are reserved so two
partners do not claim the same work, and unreachable targets enter a cooldown
instead of trapping a helper in a repeated path attempt.

Eligible partners can also be mounted. Filter the Master List's **Rideable**
column to see the current 95 mounts. New mounts include Corviknight, Drampa,
Revavroom, Volcarona, Houndstone, Cresselia, Great Tusk, Roaring Moon,
Walking Wake, Iron Leaves, Slither Wing, Stakataka, and Sneasler.
Regional forms inherit a regular counterpart's mount profile unless they have
their own. Galarian Zapdos has a ground-running profile with no water crossing.
Sneasler provides ground travel; it does not climb cliffs.
Swimming and flying mounts can cross water,
but they still respect obstacles and character collision. A waterborne mount
cannot be dismissed while that would leave its rider stranded.

## Combat, moves, and progression

Wild Pokémon, Stardew monsters, story opponents, visiting trainers, and bosses
all use the same core type, category, defense, status, and move systems.

Pokémon learn moves as they level. Open the Journal to choose the equipped set.
Physical moves use Defense, special moves use Special Defense, and status moves
can heal, shield, empower, weaken, poison, burn, slow, stun, freeze, or change
combat tempo. Every move also has its own cooldown, followed by a shared
post-action recovery.

Attack, Special Attack, Defense, Special Defense, and Speed changes use stages.
Each successful application lasts 30 seconds and stacks with earlier uses up to
six stages in either direction; every stack keeps its own expiration time. A
move can change several stats at once, so Shell Smash applies both defensive
drops and all three offensive/speed boosts from one effect roll. Cyan rising
arrows mark increases, while yellow falling arrows and a stat abbreviation mark
decreases. Weather setters and pure accuracy/evasion status moves are not part
of the playable move library. Damaging or mixed moves such as Mud-Slap and Hone
Claws remain available, but their accuracy/evasion components are omitted.

### How combat decisions work

Combat stance answers *whether* a partner should engage; its equipped moves
answer *how*. Defensive partners answer nearby threats after combat begins.
Aggressive partners seek valid targets. Catching stance preserves wild targets
at 10% HP for capture instead of finishing them. At the floor, attacks continue
without damage so safe capture effects such as Sleep, Paralysis, and Freeze can
still land; Poison and Burn do not. Peaceful partners ignore ordinary monsters
and wild Pokémon while still participating in scripted trainer, rival, and boss
battles.

The combat system keeps several resources separate:

- **HP** determines whether a Pokémon can remain deployed;
- **energy** fuels field work, not battle moves, although extreme tiredness
  slows movement and move recovery;
- **move cooldowns** limit each move, with a short shared recovery preventing a
  larger equipped move set from becoming extra attack speed; and
- **ailments and buffs** change control, damage, defense, speed, or recovery
  until they expire or are cured.

Type effectiveness is only one part of a good matchup. Physical and special
moves test different defenses, short-range moves need a safe path to connect,
and status moves can create the opening for a difficult catch. Against a boss,
watch the ground telegraphs first; damage during a safe window is more valuable
than forcing an attack through a marked danger area.

### The three skills

| Skill | Main sources | Important rewards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Training | First sightings and catches, chores, affection, evolution | Capture bonuses, encounter tools, core Stones, Slates, Rare Candy, doubled shiny odds at level 10 |
| Battling | Credited victories and first-species victory bonuses | Damage and battle XP, medicines, battle catalysts, Trophy Hunter, boxed party share, Rally |
| Breeding | Producing and hatching Eggs | Faster readiness and hatching, Candy, nursery capacity, breeding catalysts, inherited traits |

At level 5 and level 10, each skill offers professions. Read the profession
cards before choosing because they shape different play styles. Skill levels,
milestones, and ordinary Pokémon levels are separate progress tracks.

The 1.1.6 curves are deliberately longer so routine play cannot grant several
early levels at once. **Mod Options → Progression → Pokémon skill XP rate**
scales all three skill awards from 25 to 200 percent without changing existing
XP. The Journal's Skills page is the complete in-game perk and profession
reference and also offers the 10,000g profession retraining described above.
Fractional awards from the XP-rate setting carry forward per farmer and skill,
so four one-point awards at 25 percent correctly become one XP instead of being
rounded away.

Training advances mainly through discovery, catching, affection, evolution,
and a limited amount of daily chore credit. Battling advances when an owned
Pokémon receives credit for victories, with extra value for the first victory
against a species. Breeding advances through pair and Egg progression. Merely
throwing a Ball, opening menus, changing moves, or dealing damage without a
victory does not replace the intended activity for those skills.

Milestones run alongside skill levels. They reward collection breadth, distinct
victories, hatched family roots, and research accomplishments with permanent
benefits. Check both Journal pages when planning the next goal: a milestone may
be closer and more useful than the next skill level.

## Evolution and breeding

The Journal's Evolve action lists every currently valid route. Natural routes
can depend on level, bond, known moves, time, location, form, gender, or other
conditions.

The five core Evolution Stones and 24 additional catalysts are shortcuts, not
gates, for the built-in roster. A Pokémon can still reach the same form through
its authored natural route. Hold the correct item, make the intended Pokémon
active, and use Action/Check to confirm the immediate evolution.

### Breeding in a Barn

Press `B` on keyboard or `RB` on controller while standing inside a Barn. A
pair needs two free animal slots. Use **Breeding: On/Off** to choose whether
its residents produce Eggs. When breeding is off, any two different available
Pokémon can live in the Barn regardless of gender or whether their species can
breed; they still receive care, roam, and produce Barn goods, but readiness and
Egg production pause. When breeding is on, the normal pairing rule is one
female and one male, or at least one Ditto. Bond improves compatibility and
readiness but does not gate a valid pair. The menu supports stick or D-pad
navigation, Confirm to choose, and Cancel to close.

1. Assign a pair in the Barn menu.
2. Care for the resident parents and allow readiness to reach 100.
3. Pick up the Egg from the Barn floor or collect its protected waiting copy.
4. Carry the Egg while walking, or leave it for passive Barn incubation after
   that feature unlocks.
5. Hatch the Egg into the party or personal storage.

Eggs snapshot species, form, temperament, ability, compatible inherited moves,
shiny state, hatch requirement, level, and starting bond when produced.
The Egg preview labels the expected family as already hatched or **NEW** for
the family checklist. Favorite Pokémon, including favorite Dittos, stay pinned
ahead of non-favorites in every parent sort. A resident parent can be replaced
directly without ending the pair; readiness restarts, but protected waiting
Eggs remain. After ending a pair, the Barn remembers both parents so reopening
the menu can repeat that pairing without another box search.

## Research, requests, and exploration

### How quests work

Pelipper Town quests arrive in several forms. Heart stories begin from an NPC
event or location visit, mail favors wait in the mailbox until accepted, and
Pokémon request-board jobs use Stardew's normal Help Wanted deadline. The
Journal records active objectives, while letters and the in-game text history
preserve clues such as encounter locations.

Story encounters usually accept either a catch or a defeat. Catching advances
the same objective and sends the Pokémon to the party or personal storage;
trainer-owned opponents, such as Alex's team, are battles only. A story target
that is described as persistent can be revisited after leaving the map or
waiting for another eligible day, so a failed first attempt does not require a
new save. Quest lures and summon items are consumed only after their encounter
is created successfully.

Read donation and return choices carefully. A donated Pokémon becomes a
permanent villager resident, and some stories deliberately ask whether to give
up or keep a unique Pokémon. The confirmation screen blocks unsafe choices,
but it does not turn a confirmed donation into a temporary loan.

In multiplayer, most friendship requirements and personal quest rewards belong
to the farmer who started the quest. The host owns shared world encounters and
story actors, so nearby farmhands can help resolve a fight without receiving a
second copy of the quest reward. The full spoiler table below identifies each
story's trigger, objective, and result.

### Fossils

Break rocks or hoe fresh soil to find one of nine fossils. Donating one to
Gunther admits that family to ordinary wild tables when fossil research is
enabled. Gunther also names the best sighting area, guarantees the first
research sighting, and gives the family eight times its normal encounter weight
for the donation day and the following day.

The nine families are Omanyte, Kabuto, Aerodactyl, Lileep, Anorith, Cranidos,
Shieldon, Tyrunt, and Amaura.

### Mail favors and the request board

Willy's first Water-type donation opens the mail-favor chain. Its letter can
arrive from Spring 5 onward and remains available in later seasons and years.
Robin later asks for 25 Wood to repair the Pokémon request board. Once repaired,
the mod can add a Pokémon posting when the ordinary Help Wanted board is empty.

One-time favors can leave donated Pokémon as permanent residents, unlock free
healing or tools, award Eggs, and introduce summon relics. Letters have no
deadline. Board requests use the ordinary Help Wanted deadline.

Do not donate a favorite or unique individual casually. The donation screen
protects active, last-party, breeding, fainted, ailing, or item-carrying
Pokémon, but the selected eligible Pokémon is still permanently given away.

## Multiplayer systems

Every player should install the same Pelipper Town 1.2.1 build before joining;
this release uses multiplayer protocol 14. A mismatched build is rejected so
one player cannot silently run incompatible Pokémon state or interaction rules.

Each farmer owns their starting journey, party, active partner, skills,
milestones, gifts, and most personal quest progress. The host manages world
encounters, bosses, shared trainer ladders, and farm-wide save data.

- Right-click another online farmer, or face them and use Action/Check, to
  request a Pokémon trade or a consensual PvP battle.
- Turn off **Farmer trade & PvP interactions** in Mod Options to leave those
  action presses to Stardew and automatically decline incoming requests. This
  preference is personal to each player.
- Trades use ready and final-confirm stages. Valid trade evolutions run after
  ownership changes.
- PvP uses each farmer's healthy deployed partner, stays on the current map,
  and fully heals both parties when it ends.
- The Team Box is shared across the farm.
- The Global Box follows the local player profile between separate saves and
  farms.
- Farmhands can mount their own eligible Pokémon. The host-authoritative mount
  keeps that Pokémon's appearance, seat position, movement bonus, sounds,
  fishing behavior, and swimming or flying water traversal instead of creating
  a horse substitute.

The personal Pokémon Chest is different from both shared boxes. It belongs to
one farmer within the current save.

## Boss and legendary walkthrough

This section fully reveals boss locations, unlock requirements, levels, and
capture rules.

Every wild boss and story enemy in this section can be caught. Boss catch rates
are low, so weaken the target and use stronger Balls, accurate throws, sleep,
and Training bonuses. In a group fight, catching a member removes it from the
shared battle and counts exactly like a defeat for phase changes, quest
progress, and final guaranteed rewards. Any summoned reinforcements are
catchable too.

### Summon relic encounters

Complete the matching one-time mail favor to receive the item and recipe. A
relic is consumed only when its encounter is successfully created.

| Favor | Requirements | Relic recipe | Encounter | Capture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Patient Model for Leah | Leah 4 hearts, Spring through Fall; donate Sudowoodo | Ultra Ball + Grass Plate + Psychic Plate | Celebi, level 55 | Yes |
| A Song for the Cabin for Elliott | Elliott 7 hearts, Spring or Summer; donate Altaria | Ultra Ball + Water Plate + Psychic Plate | Suicune, level 55 | Yes |

Throw a Ball relic on open reachable land. Hold and use the Ancient Bell. These

### Darkrai's nightmare

After day 20, an unclaimed night has a two percent chance to begin Darkrai's
story with a flyover above the farm. Reach six hearts with Sebastian to learn
the **Nightmare Incense** recipe: 20 Sap, 10 Fiber, and 5 Void Essence. Hold and
use the incense before bed to guarantee the flyover that night. The deliberate
ritual takes priority over another randomly selected farm event. It can also
summon Darkrai again after the player has already caught it.

The flyover begins a three-day nightmare arc. Darkrai can appear in the Town
square after 9 PM on each of those three nights. The first two mornings also
report the farmers' nightmares. Catching Darkrai ends the current arc and stops
natural flyovers, but another Nightmare Incense can begin a new three-night
rematch arc. Defeating Darkrai gives the player another chance on the next
eligible arc night.

### Cresselia's good dreams

After day 20, Cresselia has its own two percent chance to fly over the farm on
an unclaimed night. Reach six hearts with Emily to learn the **Lunar Incense**
recipe: 20 Sap, 10 Fiber, and 5 Solar Essence. Hold and use it before bed to
guarantee Cresselia's flyover, including after Cresselia has already been
caught. Only one Darkrai or Cresselia ritual can be queued at a time.

The flyover begins a three-day good-dream arc. Cresselia can appear in the Town
square after 9 PM on each night, and the first two mornings report that each
farmer had good dreams. Cresselia remains peaceful until the player attacks it;
after that first hit, it fights as a normal capturable boss. Catching it ends
the current arc and stops natural flyovers, while Lunar Incense remains a
repeatable rematch path.

### Calyrex's farm blessing

Starting on Summer 1 of Year 1, Calyrex has a 3% chance to visit on an unclaimed
night while the farmer sleeps. It blesses the densest six-by-six crop patch,
then remains on the farm for two days as a level-45 boss. Once attacked, Calyrex
warns the farmer and places the whole party in Defensive stance. Catching it
ends the natural blessing event; while healthy and in the party, **Verdant
Crown** advances one plant-growth stage in a three-by-three patch each night
without another cutscene.

At six hearts with Caroline, her letter grants a Crown Ball and its recipe. A
Crown Ball summons level-50 Calyrex directly, using the same boss fight.

### Marshadow's umbral challenge

After the farmer has a party Pokémon above level 40, Marshadow appears on the
next unclaimed night by the mailbox. The following morning brings a broken-English
letter with an Umbral Ball and its recipe. Throw the ball at night to summon a
level-45 Marshadow boss. Its first phase calls Zorua shadows; its second phase
creates a copy of the active party Pokémon, and Marshadow can swap places with
its Zorua reinforcements.

The copied shadow is a catchable reinforcement and rolls shiny independently;
it does not inherit the active Pokémon's shiny state. It uses the summon
location's exact habitat chain plus the farmer's Training and Shiny Charm
bonuses. This means an active legendary can produce a separately catchable
shiny copy during the Marshadow battle.

Marshadow can perform every companion job. **Copycat** activates when it is
performing the same type of chore as the farmer, even at a different target.
Over two seconds, its windup and work animation build smoothly from 1× to 2×
speed, its walking bonus builds from zero to 50%, and its chore cooldown falls
from normal to half. It briefly holds that momentum between nearby targets of
the same chore type. Harvesting, watering, animal care, orchard care, and
crab-pot interactions all count. In combat, the same 1×-to-2× buildup applies
only while the farmer is actively fighting within six tiles of Marshadow.

### Primal Rivalry

Reach six hearts with both Clint and Willy. Their shared letter gives an
**Elemental Core** and teaches its recipe: one Ruby plus one Aquamarine.

Throw the Core at the Beach, on any Volcano Dungeon floor, or in the Caldera.
It summons level-60 Kyogre and Groudon together. Both are catchable. Resolve
both by catching or defeating them to receive a guaranteed Ruby and Aquamarine,
which are the materials for another Core.

Kyogre's **Primordial Sea** and Groudon's **Desolate Land** act immediately.
Deploy Kyogre to bring rain or Groudon to clear the sky across every location.
The chosen weather lasts until another weather field ability changes it.
When Kyogre's rain begins, existing outdoor farm crops and pet bowls are
watered immediately.
Midday transitions use Stardew's native weather and music paths, so normal
music, ambience, and gameplay sound cues remain available.

### The Space-Time Rift

Reach eight hearts with the Wizard. His letter gives a **Space-Time Band** and
teaches its recipe: one Diamond plus one Pearl.

Throw it outdoors. Either level-65 Dialga or Palkia appears first. At half
health, the other joins the fight. If the first is caught before half health,
the second still enters so the sequence can continue. After both are caught or
defeated, a level-70 Giratina emerges. All three are catchable.

Resolving Giratina by catch or defeat drops a Prismatic Shard and, if the
resolving player does not already carry or wear one, a wearable Champion's
Crown. The Crown can be discarded normally. Any Crown has its full effects
whenever it is worn. Those effects do not require 200 caught species or seasonal
trainer rungs. Carrying a Crown in the backpack is also enough to restore
Deoxys's meteorite.

### The hidden Joja truck Mew

Interact with the front of the delivery truck beside JojaMart in Town. A
passive level-1 Mew flees from the truck and can be caught. The encounter can
be attempted once per day. This is separate from the level-70 Mew summoned by
the Ancient Bell.

### Celestial solstice visitors

Cosmog and Cosmoem do not enter ordinary wild encounter pools. Returning
Haley's missing partner in **A Very Important Favor** awards a Cosmog Egg,
which evolves into Cosmoem at level 43. At level 53, Cosmoem evolves into
Solgaleo during the day or Lunala at night.

The final forms also visit the Beach during three-day solstice windows:

| Visitor | Dates | Daily arrival |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solgaleo | Summer 13-15 | Noon onward |
| Lunala | Winter 13-15 | 9:00 PM onward |

Each visitor is level 60 and remains peaceful until attacked. Catching one is
permanent. Defeating one spends only that day's attempt, so an uncaught visitor
can return on the next eligible day. Solgaleo's **Radiant Sun** works during
the day and Lunala's **Moonlit Wings** works at night; each gives the farmer
and party 20% more damage, 1.5 pixels per tick of movement speed, and 20%
effective health during its matching period.

### Deoxys and the Beach meteorite

Travel to the western island added to the Beach. A water-capable mount can
cross the open water. Mine the meteorite until its final hit to awaken a
level-55 Deoxys.

The meteorite is protected while the boss is active. Catching Deoxys is
permanent. A defeat resolves that day's attempt. After resolution the meteorite
can be destroyed normally. Carrying or wearing any Champion's Crown restores
the meteorite and Deoxys for one daily rematch.

### Four Signs in the Sky

Meet the Wizard and catch 40 distinct species. Read his letter to begin the
quest. Each sign appears during its season's final three days:

| Sign | Date | Location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ho-Oh | Spring 26-28 | Western pond in Cindersap Forest |
| Zapdos | Summer 26-28 | Stone platform on the eastern Beach island |
| Moltres | Fall 26-28 | Caldera at the Island Volcano Forge |
| Articuno | Winter 26-28 | Mountain lake |

The first sign you face is level 55. Each other sign already caught or defeated
adds five levels, so the last can be level 70. All four signs and Lugia remain
passive until attacked, including Crown rematches. Their boss introduction and
health bar appear only when the fight begins.

Catching a sign is permanent. Defeating one spends the rest of that seasonal
window, so an uncaught sign normally returns the following year. A calendar run therefore
takes planning across all four seasons.

After all four signs have been caught or defeated, the Wizard sends another
clue. Visit the Beach shoreline during **days 1-3 of any season** for level-75
Lugia. A defeat spends the rest of that opening window. A catch is permanent.

### Latias and Latios

Open the Four Signs letter and catch 45 distinct species. One level-70 Eon
Pokémon then roams each day.

The route cycles among the Mountain, Forest, Railroad, Beach, and Desert. While
both are uncaught, Latias and Latios alternate by day. Once one is caught, the
other becomes the active roamer every day.

Reach six hearts with Emily, learn the Great Ball recipe, and unlock the day's
area to receive a morning clue naming the location. Defeating the active twin
spends the day. Catching it is permanent.

### Mewtwo

Reach displayed floor 99 of Skull Cavern. Shaft falls are clamped so they
cannot skip the encounter floor. A level-100 Mewtwo appears there.

Catching Mewtwo is permanent. Defeating it spends the day. While a Champion's
Crown is worn, it can return there as a daily landmark rematch.

### The Champion's Crown

The normal achievement route for receiving a Crown requires:

1. The farm has caught at least 200 distinct species.
2. That farmer has completed all three visiting-trainer rungs in Spring,
   Summer, Fall, and Winter.

The twelve seasonal rungs can be completed across different years. Giratina is
a second acquisition route and drops the same functional item when the resolving
player does not already carry or wear one. The Crown can be placed in the
inventory trash slot. It never checks caught-species totals or trainer-rung
progress when applying its effects.

Wear the Crown to restore one resolved landmark encounter per species per day.
This covers the Four Signs, Lugia, the five lesser Regis, Mewtwo, Latias,
Latios, and Arceus. Deoxys also responds while the Crown is merely carried.
Relic summons still require their consumable items because they have no fixed
landmark.

Each nonshiny Crown rematch resolved by catch or defeat builds one
species-specific Legendary Resonance roll, up to 30 stored bonus rolls. A shiny
resolution resets that species' Resonance.

### Arceus

Wear a Champion's Crown and enter the Summit. A capturable level-100
Arceus appears. Its direct catch rate is 1 percent before normal bonuses.

Arceus returns as a daily Crown challenge until resolved for that day. It is
the final fixed encounter in the current progression.

#### Mega Evolution

The built-in roster currently contains 669 form definitions representing 638
unique National Pokédex species. SpriteCollab tracks canonical Mega forms for
59 of those species (61 forms because Charizard and Mewtwo each have two), but
only forms with usable directional overworld art are enabled. Twenty-seven use
complete SpriteCollab sheets; Mega Slowbro, Mega Sceptile, and Mega Scrafty use
partial sheets with explicit fallbacks for their missing runtime animations.
The remaining 31 tracked forms have portraits but no usable overworld sheet in
the pinned source revision.

Raise any owned Pokémon to level 50. Mayor Lewis will send a letter containing
the reusable **Mega Evolution Guide** and the **Mega Band** recipe he found in
the Mayor's Manor basement. The recipe costs 1 Iridium Bar and 1 Prismatic
Shard. Deploy a compatible active Pokémon, hold the band, and press
Action/Check. Each farmer can begin one Mega Evolution per
in-game day. The form lasts until bedtime and cannot be manually toggled off;
the Pokémon returns to its Ball when the form ends, or reverts sooner if it
faints. Only one of a farmer's partners can remain Mega Evolved at a time, so
another cannot transform until the current Mega has fainted. Mega Evolution
preserves the Pokémon's current HP percentage and grants 25% more maximum HP
and battle stats, 15% faster field movement, and a 1.5x-strength passive. Mega
Alakazam offers Teleport at 1:30 AM, Mega
Kangaskhan shortens animal maturity by two days, Mega Absol can trigger

### Settings and accessibility

Generic Mod Config Menu is optional. Without it, the same values can be edited
in the generated `config.json`.

#### Wild Pokémon

- enable wild encounters and dungeon tables separately;
- allow ordinary wild Pokémon on the farm and in Pelican Town separately, so
  either area can stay clear without hiding owned Pokémon or scripted encounters;
- opt individual Generations 1–9 into ordinary wild encounters independently
  from the ambient villager-partner pool, without changing owned Pokémon,
  evolutions, quests, trainer battles, or scripted and legendary encounters;
- tune refill chance plus maximum unzoned-map and per-habitat populations;
  lower the habitat limit to 1–3 on low-end systems;
- enable or disable first-year scaling;
- require fossil research or allow fossil families from the beginning;
- choose Peaceful, Defensive, Authored, or Aggressive wild behavior;
- optionally make boss and legendary area attacks aim at deployed Pokémon
  before falling back to farmers;
- toggle catchable Magikarp and the DexNav arrows; and
- set wild sound volume.

#### Capture

- unlimited Poké Balls (the host's value controls the whole multiplayer farm);
- overall catch multiplier;
- technique bonuses;
- auto-lock, homing, maximum range, and in-flight steering.

#### Companions

- health, fainting, stamina, combat, ailments, and passives independently;
- compact or full partner HUD meters above/left of the live player bars, or in
  the legacy top-left position; both sizes keep the native bar width and compact
  changes only their height, plus evolution reminders;
- harmless versus destructive chores;
- species job restrictions and field-skill ceilings;
- companion sound, experience, and sale-value multipliers;
- keyboard and controller bindings.

#### Appearance

- choose original Pokémon contours, optimized soft contours with a 128 MiB
  session budget, or uncapped normal soft contours;
- optionally apply the same treatment to custom item sprites; and
- scale Journey Setup, Partner Journal, and custom hover text from 100–150%.

#### Villagers

Every supported social NPC has authored choices. Marriage candidates have five,
other built-in villagers have at least three, and Pam has a fourth option for
Purugly. The Custom option accepts any loaded species name or ID with an
independent shiny toggle. Compatible social NPCs from other mods appear in an
additional-NPC section.

Each NPC also has a **Companion enabled** toggle. Turning it off hides that
villager's roaming partner without discarding the configured species or shiny
choice, so the same companion returns when the toggle is enabled again.

Turn off **Pokémon say their names** for a lore-friendly greeting mode.
Interacting with a villager's Pokémon then uses a stable broad creature profile
to choose one of five chirps, growls, trills, hisses, croaks, mechanical noises,
and other name-free calls. There are 22 profiles, with specific voices for bats,
primates, bears, horses, other hoofed Pokémon, crustaceans, and amorphous or
slime-like Pokémon alongside the elemental fallbacks. The normal once-per-day
greeting is still recorded. When the option is on, full-name lines remain more
common, but five shortened-name rhythms are mixed in, such as Pikachu using
Pika-pi or Pika-chu.

The **Villager partner generations** checkboxes keep every configured choice
unchanged, but a roaming partner whose generation is disabled uses an authored
or curated eligible replacement matched to that character's normal partner
themes. Re-enabling the configured species' generation restores it. With only
Generation 1 selected, villagers retain the original distinct, curated Kanto
assignments from Gen 1 world mode.

### Troubleshooting

#### I cannot find a species

Check **Partner Journal → Wild Pokémon** for its area and current-year status.
Then consider season, time, weather, terrain, dungeon depth, fossil research,
and whether wild encounters are enabled.

#### My party is full

The catch goes to that farmer's Pokémon Chest. Place or open any Pokémon Chest
to reach the same stored roster.

#### An item does nothing

Most Pokémon items are active-use objects. Put it in the hotbar, select the
intended active partner when relevant, and press Stardew's Action/Check button.
Encounter lures require a valid outdoor area and refuse invalid use without
consuming the item.

#### I cannot give a Pokémon item to an NPC

Only replaceable supplies are giftable. Progression, museum, Egg, quest, summon,
tool, furniture, and wearable items are protected.

#### A villager's preferred Slate changed

Slate and Evolution Stone tastes follow the partner currently selected for that
NPC in Mod Options. Changing or customizing the partner changes those tastes.

#### A farmhand sees different progress

Parties, skills, gifts, milestones, and several unlocks are farmer-specific.
The host handles shared world encounters, museum state, and farm-wide save
data.
